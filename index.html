Introduction

My name is YI.From an early age, the enchanting hues of the evening sky and the boundless, crystalline expanse of daylight left an indelible mark on my consciousness. These captivating vistas have remained etched in my memory, compelling me to capture the myriad beautiful moments in life. As I grew older, I got my first camera and embarked on my journey in photography. Through the lens, I found a medium to articulate my emotions and present my unique perspective. Although I have not received formal training in photography, my fervor for capturing life’s essence through this art form has never diminished. To further refine my skills and better articulate my ideas, I aspire to deepen my understanding of photography in my future studies

For life

Everyday photography is a gentle gaze, a rediscovery of ordinary life. It does not pursue grand narratives or perfect compositions, but captures those easily overlooked moments.An ordinary person's photography diary: Collecting life with a lens I am just an ordinary person who likes to take pictures. There are no stunning landscapes or elaborate portraits in my camera, but only those moments in life that make me want to press the shutter - because they are real, because they are mine. For me, some things are more precious than any carefully designed work. My lens is like a visual diary, clumsily but honestly preserving the world in my eyes. Sometimes it is the grass in the park, sometimes it is the snow under the flash, and sometimes it is the capture of a friend's thinking. I don't understand the profound composition theory, I just simply think "this scene is so good", so I raise my phone or camera. It doesn't matter if the picture is blurry, and it doesn't matter if the light and shadow are dim - these imperfect pictures make the memory more vivid. For me, taking pictures has never been about creating any works of art, but a gentle ritual of life. When I organized these scattered fragments into an album, it was like repeatedly confirming: it turns out that there are so many glimmers of light worth remembering hidden in my ordinary days.

When photography enters the field of art, its possibilities of subjective expression are infinitely expanded. Edward Weston's close-up of peppers makes vegetables look like human curves, and Hiroshi Sugimoto's long exposure of seascapes makes water present a meditative texture. These works prove that the lens can reshape the meaning of the material world. Contemporary artist Wolfgang Tillmans challenges the dogma that photography must be "perfectly clear" by deliberately developing flaws and folding photos. This creative freedom reveals an essence: photography has never been an absolutely objective medium, and the moment the shutter is pressed already contains the photographer's understanding and intervention of the world.

1396 Square Metre

This project embarks on an explorative journey into the lives of several Chinese students in Canada. It starts by retrospecting personal narratives and moving beyond conventional documentary approaches, the work synthesizes portraiture, environmental photography, and MBTI analysis to map the intersection of educational displacement, academic endeavours, personality archetypes, social dynamics, and cultural negotiation. At its core, this project is deeply personal and captures everyday life, rooted in my experiences situating in the Western educational system while preserving cultural identity, profoundly resonating with the challenges faced by international students. Driven by a growing self-awareness from teenage years to early youth, the transition from the rigid structures of Chinese education to Western education encouraging critical thinking, creativity, and individual expression prompts a reevaluation of one's self-identity, beliefs, and cultural background, all of which are crucial elements in the narrative of a Chinese student's growth in Canada. In addition, this project expands contemporary discourse on educational migration and diaspora identity by introducing typological analysis to visual anthropology methodologies, weaving together individual growth while highlighting how different personalities navigate the same challenges. By incorporating MBTI personality analysis, I delve into the students' traits and inner emotions, correlating their cognitive processing styles (as per Jungian typology) with observed adaptation strategies, enriching and diversifying the understanding of international student life for a wider audience. Through contextualizing Canadian environments with typological portraiture, I hope to contribute to a broader yet nuanced conversation about the international student experience, shedding light on the intricate interplay between educational systems, intercultural communication, and personal development.

Inspiration

This documentary, through interviews with 2,000 women from 50 countries, explores the lives of women worldwide. It inspired me to narrate and present my own high school experiences, while investigating the lives and challenges of fellow female international students in the context of Canada. In the film, the directors allow women to speak directly in front of the camera. I adopted a similar approach—using close-up shots to capture the facial expressions of the women.
Richard Avedon's black-and-white static portrait works often involved long conversations with his models during the shoot, allowing him to capture their rich emotions when they were relaxed. In order to help my friends relax in front of the camera, I adopted Avedon's technique of engaging in prolonged conversations to ease their discomfort. To keep the visuals simple and emphasize emotions, I also used black-and-white imagery to present my friends.
Alec Soth's works often use photography to tell stories, and his works inspired me to capture school and daily life scenes when narrating my high school experiences.

Final work(part)

This is the living environment I live in. Studying abroad in Canada is like a prism, reflecting youth in multiple spectra: it has the anxiety of staying up late to meet deadlines, the fragility of being alone and homesick, as well as the richness of cultural hybridity and the ecstasy of self-breakthrough. This complex survival experience ultimately points to an ultimate proposition: in the era of global mobility, studying abroad is not only a path to obtain a diploma, but also a rite of passage to reshape life resilience and worldview. For policymakers and educational institutions, how to build a more supportive ecosystem for international students still needs to be explored.

Girl's Though

Adolescence marks a pivotal chapter in a woman's journey of becoming. These years pulse with a psychological landscape both intricate and extraordinary - where bodily transformations intertwine with the quiet revolution of self-discovery. Each fleeting emotion, each conflicted thought becomes a brushstroke painting the profound metamorphosis from girlhood to womanhood, as she navigates the shifting tides between who she is and who the world expects her to be.

Inspiration

Through Julian’s lens, the models often convey a sense of relaxation and calm, as if the medium of photography dissolves the opposing dynamics between the photographer and the subject, bringing them closer and making the models appear very natural in front of the camera. Therefore, I also attempted to present this warm visual texture, expressing my own reinterpretation of childhood experiences

In this collection of works by Yukihiko Ueda, the darker scenes combined with soft lighting create a tranquil atmosphere and texture, allowing the model’s emotions and feelings to be directly conveyed to me, which sparked my visual inspiration. As a result, I also attempted to use natural light in dim settings, focusing on the effect of creating atmosphere.
